# The 4 Voices — Perspectives  

*From the book [Pits, Mits, Klop, Laram](https://www.amazon.de/dp/618005228X) by Nikos Markopoulos.*  

## 14 - Me in me, me out of me, me with all, all in me.  

*Subjectivity, Objectivity, Togetherness and Spirituality.*  

Each of my Subpersonalities has their own unique way of interpreting information. My past experiences, beliefs, and values heavily influence how my alters make sense of sensory information. Pits focuses on the emotional or subjective aspects of things, Mits prioritizes logic and objectivity, Klop builds connections and relationships, while Laram connects with the divine and the spiritual self.

### Me in me

> “I observe myself from the inside.” - Pits

Pits represents subjectivity, the perception of the internal. His perception refers to the personal and individualized nature of his thoughts, feelings, or opinions. It is influenced by his perspective, beliefs and emotions, which shape the way he interprets and understands the world around him. Pits is your subjective self.

### Me out of me

> "I see the world outside of myself, and I observe myself from the outside." - Mits

Mits stands for objectivity, which refers to the perception of the external. Mits has the ability to perceive the external world in a neutral and factual manner, without personal opinions, feelings, or biases. She looks at things objectively, ensuring that subjectivity does not cloud her judgment or influence her perception. Mits is your objective self.

### Me with all

> "I realize that I am not an individual; rather, I am a part of everything around me. I expand, and I see myself as an integral part of the whole.” - Klop

Klop stands for togetherness, the perception of being united, connected, or in harmony with others. She is the part of me that unifies, connects, and creates bridges for communication. She has a sense of belonging, mutual support, understanding, and shared experiences, creating strong relationships. Klop is your connecting self.

### All in me

> “I breathe the whole world into myself. I absorb the world around me and unify it with my being. I lead. I am aware of my mission to make this world a better place.” - Laram

Laram represents spirituality and the perception of the non-physical dimensions of life. He believes in something greater than himself and a connection to a higher power or universal energy. He possesses a sense of purpose, meaning, and inner peace, as well as actively practices meditation and self-reflection. Laram is your spiritual self.

## 15 - Imagine, think, connect, remember.

*Imagining self, Thinking self, Connecting self and Remembering self.*

> “When you imagine, feel, and think, you build connections which create memories.” - Baskaboo

### Pits imagines

Pits is your emotional and imagining self and stands for subjectivity. He represents the emotional and imaginative aspects of your personality. This includes the ability to feel, visualise, dream, and think outside the box. Subjectivity plays a significant role in shaping and influencing your emotional and imagining self, as it colors the way you perceive, interpret, and emotionally engage with your internal mental images and scenarios.

### Mits thinks

Mits is your thinking self and stands for objectivity. She relates to logical thinking, rationality, and analysis and focuses on facts, evidence, and rational decision-making. She enables you to analyze and evaluate information using logical reasoning, which helps you make objective judgments and decisions. In other words, your thinking self allows you to think objectively and remove personal biases when engaging with the world.

### Klop connects

Klop is your connecting self and stands for togetherness. She represents your ability to form and maintain relationships, establish communication, and empathize with others. She includes social skills, empathy, and a sense of belonging. She forms connections and relationships with others and offers you a feeling of togetherness. When your connecting self is strong, you have a powerful sense of togetherness and feel connected to others in a meaningful way. Your connecting self acts as a bridge between your imagining and thinking selves.

### Laram remembers

He is your remembering self and stands for spirituality. Laram is associated with the divine. He relates to the deeper sense of self and your connection to something greater than yourself. This connection includes qualities such as contemplation, self-reflection, and the search for meaning and purpose in life. Laram connects with the universal memory, which is the collective storehouse of knowledge, experiences, and memories that every entity, thing, or idea can access. Your remembering self stores the memories of your emotional, thinking, and connecting self and uses them to power the continuity of your existence.

> “When you live, you are what you remember. After you die, you are what others remember about you.” - Baskaboo
